4.2. Enhancing Motion Expressiveness
While our model enables real-time motion generation, we
observe that it struggles to produce expressive motions cru-
cial for natural human conversation. In contrast to achieving
an accurate lip-sync that has an almost one-to-one match
with the avatar audio, appropriate reaction to the user’s mo-
tion and audio is highly ambiguous, as there is no single
correct response [33, 35, 54].
Moreover, as shown in Fig. 5, existing listening datasets
generally exhibit lower motion expressiveness compared to
speaking datasets, which leads models to learn passive and
non-expressive listening behaviors. While prior work at-
tempted to address it with personalized motion [35] or ex-
ternal text instructions [33], these methods do not address
the core challenge of learning the true interactive behavior
for natural interaction.

mulate this as an alignment problem and apply the Rein-

user motion, and the generated video, which are difficult to

We adapted a reward-free method, Direct Preference Op-
timization (DPO) [41, 52], for fine-tuning our motion gener-
ation based on diffusion forcing. Specifically, we construct
preference pairs of motion latents (mw, ml) as follows:
• Preferred sample mw: the motion latent from the
ground-truth video, exhibiting expressive and contextu-
ally appropriate responses.
• Less preferred sample ml: the motion latent generated
by a separately trained talking avatar model [24], con-
ditioned solely on the avatar audio.
This paired design yields a clear signal for enhancing ex-
pressiveness, emphasizing active listening and reactive mo-
tion while leaving other aspects, such as lip sync or speech-
driven motion, unchanged.
Building on the original DPO objective LDPO (Eq. (2))
with our constructed pairs (mw, ml), we fine-tune our dif-
fusion forcing model vθ with the following objective:
