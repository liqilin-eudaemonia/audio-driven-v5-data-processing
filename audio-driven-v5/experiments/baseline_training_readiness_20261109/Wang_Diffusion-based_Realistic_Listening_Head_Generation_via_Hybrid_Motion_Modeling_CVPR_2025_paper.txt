Abstract
Listening head generation aims to synthesize non-verbal
responsive listening head videos that naturally react to a
certain speaker, for which, both realistic head movements,
expressive facial expressions, and high visual qualities are
expected. Previous approaches typically follow a two-stage
pipeline that first generates intermediate 3D motion signals
such as 3DMM coefficients, and then synthesizes the videos
by deterministic rendering, suffering from limited motion
expressiveness and low visual quality (e.g. 256 × 256). In
this work, we propose a novel listening head generation
method that harnesses the generative capabilities of the dif-
fusion model for both motion generation and high-quality
rendering. Crucially, we propose an effective hybrid motion
modeling module that addresses training difficulties caused
by the scarcity of listening head data while preserving the
intricate details that may be lost in explicit motion repre-
sentations. We further develop a tailored control guidance
for head pose and facial expression, by integrating their in-
trinsic motion characteristics. Our method enables high-
fidelity video generation with 512 × 512 resolution and de-
livers vivid listener motion feedback. We conduct compre-
hensive experiments and obtain superior performance in
terms of both visual quality and motion expressiveness com-
pared with existing methods.
1. Introduction
Listening head generation aims to generate responsive lis-
tener videos that react to the corresponding speaker [46].
Unlike talking head videos, the listener’s response mainly
consists of non-verbal behaviors, such as nodding, smil-

of the listener feedback are vital to the success of speaker
and listener communication. Being a crucial part of digital
avatar generation, listening head generation has many po-
tential applications, such as movie production, online cus-
tomer service, and human-computer interaction.
While most works focus on speaker-centric talking head
generation [16, 17, 26–28, 37, 44], only a few study lis-
tening head generation [21, 24, 25, 46]. It is a non-trivial
problem as given a certain speaker video, there can be many
reasonable listener reactions. Such a one-to-many mapping
problem makes it challenging for the model to learn to gen-
erate proper yet diverse listener motions. In addition, since
the generated listening head mostly contains non-verbal re-
actions, the audience may focus more on the listeners’ facial
expressions, requiring that the generated reaction videos be
high-fidelity to manifest detailed and subtle expressions.
The state-of-the-art works of listening head generation
follow a two-stage design that first generates intermediate
listener motion signals and then synthesizes the RGB videos
with post-rendering [20, 21, 35, 46]. For motion generation,
explicit 3D motion signals, such as 3DMM [3] coefficients
are used as motion descriptions. Existing works developed
different models to generate the listener’s 3D motion coeffi-
cients, including LSTM [46], VQ-VAE [35], and diffusion-
based models [20, 21]. As for the video rendering, PIRen-
derer [29] is used to generate the RGB frames conditioned
on the generated 3DMM coefficients. Though promising,
they still fall short of generating expressive and natural lis-
tening head motions, as well as photorealistic rendering.
For instance, 1) the intermediate parametric 3D motion sig-
nals, such as the 3DMM expression coefficients, are char-
acterized in a low-dimensional generalizable subspace and
lack facial details and subtle expressions, which limits the
richness and naturalness of the learned facial expressions, 2)
while focusing more on motion generation, the determinis-

tic rendering used in existing works is of low visual quality,
suffering from low resolution (e.g. 256 × 256), identity in-

In this work, we propose a novel listening head gener-
ation framework that enables both highly expressive head
motions and photorealistic rendering. Our motivation is to
fully leverage the generative ability of the diffusion model
for both motion generation and video synthesis. Recently,
the latent diffusion model (LDM) [30] has shown supe-
rior ability in high-resolution and vivid video generation,
largely promoting the speaker-centric talking head genera-
tion performance [7, 34, 41]. Nevertheless, it is not feasi-
ble to directly apply these methods to listening head gen-
eration.
Firstly, they require hundreds of hours of talk-
ing head videos for training the audio-to-visual correspon-
dence, which does not apply to listening head generation,
where only a few hours of training data are available. Sec-
ondly, due to the high computational and memory cost,
these methods can only model the context information in
a small window size (e.g. 16 frames in [34]). This is accept-
able for talking head generation since talking heads have a
strong correlation with the audio. However, for listening
head generation, the listener feedback may be related to an
even longer duration of the speaker’s content. To generate
a reasonable reaction, it may require long-term modeling
of audio conditions and motion. Based on these, it is non-
trivial to unleash the diffusion generative ability for listen-
ing head generation.
To address these challenges, we propose a novel hybrid
motion modeling method, which consists of an explicit and
explainable 3D motion representation and a learnable im-
plicit latent representation. Specifically, instead of utiliz-
ing the LDM-based rendering to generate listening video
directly from the speaker’s input, we first learn explicit
3D motion signals by a diffusion-based motion generation
module. The explicit motions are later used as guiding con-
ditions to train the rendering module. To facilitate controlla-
bility, we propose distinct control strategies considering the
different intrinsic natures of head poses and facial expres-
sions. The explicit motion generation module is expected
to generate feasible and diverse listener motions but may
lack facial details, as analyzed above. We further develop
an implicit motion refinement module to complement the
potentially missing facial details. With these designs, we
successfully train the LDM-based rendering model on the
small-scale listening dataset, enabling high-fidelity render-
ing (e.g. 512×512) and vivid and expressive listener motion
generation. Compared with the existing methods, we signif-
icantly boost listening head generation performance in both
visual and motion quality.
The hybrid motion design brings several advantages, 1)
the explicit motion generation module is lightweight and
takes much less computational resources than directly mod-
eling audio-to-visual correspondence, making it possible to
learn the long-term relations between speaker and listener,
which is essential for listener motion modeling.
2) The
learning of the hybrid motion representation is much easier
than the pure latent motion modeling, especially on limited
training data. In addition, with the intermediate explicit rep-
resentation, we can better utilize talking videos to pre-train
the rendering module. We conduct thorough ablation stud-
ies and verify the necessity of the hybrid motion modeling.
Our contributions are as follows,
• We propose a novel diffusion-based method for motion
generation and visual rendering, resulting in realistic and
vivid listening head generation.
• We introduce an effective hybrid motion modeling mod-
ule that overcomes training challenges while enhancing
both motion dynamics and visual quality.
• We conduct comprehensive experiments and significantly
improve listening head generation in both visual quality
and motion quality compared with existing methods.
2. Related Works
Non-verbal Listening Head Generation. Non-verbal lis-
tening head generation is an important part of digital avatar
generation, and has drawn increasing research interests in
recent years. Earlier works [4, 5, 10, 15, 32] use rule-based
methods to generate the listener response. Such methods
require specifically designed rules and pre-defined reaction
motion sequences. Thus they have very limited motion di-
versity and expressiveness.
Learning-based methods alleviate the problem by using
data-driven approaches to automatically learn to generate
proper reactions given speaker conditions. Zhou et al. [46]
propose an LSTM-based network RLHG that predicts the
listener’s head poses and 3DMM expression coefficients
given speaker’s audio and motions. Later, several works are
proposed to improve the performance of RLHG by refining
the conditional features [6, 45] or improving visual qual-
ity [14]. However, these works are deterministic models
and are inefficient in addressing the one-to-many mapping
problems in listener motion generation, resulting in unnat-
ural motions. To alleviate this, the work of [25] models the
listener motion with VQ-VAE. Song et al. [31] proposes a
more fine-grained codebook that supports emotion control.
Liu et al. [20] further improves the diversity of the gener-
ated motion by a diffusion model. In addition, the work of
Liu et al. [21] improves the controllability of the diffusion-
based motion generation model by aggregating the user-
specified expression and pose labels. Focusing on interme-
diate motion generation, these methods suffer from limited
expressiveness and naturalness in the generated videos.
Diffusion Generative Models on Head Generation. De-
noising diffusion probabilistic models [13] have shown re-
markable results on image generation tasks. An iconic work

3. Method
Given a single listener portrait image and a speaker’s audio
and video, we aim to generate a non-verbal responsive lis-
tening head video that synchronizes with the speaker, with
detailed facial expressions and natural head movements.

model [13, 30], which is a key component of our frame-
work.

3.2. Network Pipeline
Let A ∈RN×da and S ∈RN×d denote the speaker’s au-
dio and 3D head motion features, where N is the number of
frames, da and d are dimensionality of audio and speaker’s
motion features respectively. Let I ∈Rh×w×c be the lis-
tener portrait for identity reference. As shown in Fig. 1,
to enable high-fidelity rendering and vivid responsive head
motion generation, 1) we adopt an LDM-based high-fidelity
rendering module to harness the power of diffusion models,

so the visual qualities of the generated videos can be greatly
improved. 2) We propose a novel hybrid motion modeling
module that consists of a lightweight explicit motion gener-
ation module to generate explicit guiding motions, i.e. head
poses and facial expressions, a tailored control strategy for
the rigid head poses and non-rigid facial expressions, and
an implicit and learnable motion refinement module to com-
plement the motion details. 3) To enhance the identity con-
sistency of the generated video, a parallel reference net with
similar structure as the rendering module is used to integrate
the listener identity features. We introduce each part next.
High-fidelity Rendering Module. One major limitation of
the existing listening head generation methods is the deter-
ministic rendering module such as PIRenderer [29], which
suffers from low resolution, identity inconsistency, and vi-
sual artifacts. Inspired by the recent advance in diffusion-
based talking head generation works [34, 36, 39], we de-
sign a high-fidelity rendering module based on SD 1.5. To
enable the video generation capability of SD 1.5, we follow
the motion module design [11, 19] that applies self attention
along the temporal dimension of the network’s intermediate
features, ensuring the continuity and consistency along the
generated video frames. Benefiting from the probabilistic
nature and the great generative capabilities, the proposed
rendering module can generate photorealistic videos with
detailed visuals and expressive motions.
Conditioned on reference image I, and speaker features
A and S, the rendering module R takes noised latent Vt ∈
RN×h×w×c at step t as inputs, and predicts the denoised
latent ˆV0:

Explicit Motion Generation.
We design a transformer
diffusion-based explicit motion generation module, inspired
by the successful applications of diffusion models on vari-
ous human motion generations [1, 21, 33, 42]. The motion
generation module generates both listener’s facial expres-
sions and head poses with a lightweight diffusion network,
addressing the one-to-many mapping problem as well as en-
abling us to model motions in larger time window.
Given a noised motion sequence Lt ∈RM×d at step t,
the explicit motion generation module G aims to predict the
clean sample ˆL condition on speaker features A and S:
ˆL = G(Lt, t, c), c = {A, S}.
(5)
As shown in Fig. 1 (a), the noised motion sequence Lt is
mapped to the latent space and then encoded by a trans-
former encoder. In the meantime, the speaker features, i.e.
speakers facial expression features and audio features are
integrated by a cross-modal transformer, where we use a
pretrained speech model wav2vec 2.0 [2] to encode the raw
audio. Then a transformer decoder is used to generate de-
noised sequence, where the integrated speaker features are
used as keys and values.
Explicit Motion Guidance. ˆL generated by Eq. (5) con-
tains the learned explicit 3D head poses and facial expres-
sion coefficients. It serves as a explicit motion guidance in
our design. There are other options for explicit motion rep-
resentation such as mesh, normal maps etc. We choose ex-
pression coefficients because it’s more compact and seman-
tic meaningful. The ways to inject conditional information
are crucial in diffusion models. An intuitive way to add con-
trollability of head poses and facial expressions might be
concatenating these features and injecting to the layers of
the rendering module via cross-attention operator, which is
a common practice in existing methods. However, in prac-
tice, such an operation leads to sub-optimal head pose and
expression controllability. Considering the different intrin-
sic nature of head poses and facial expressions, i.e. head
pose is a global rigid transformation that decides the posi-
tion of the generated head while facial expression is a lo-
cal non-rigid transformation that focuses more on the visual
details, we opt to use distinct conditional strategies for head
pose and facial expressions accordingly.

pose guidance images by connecting them. Then, a pose
guidance net consists of multiple convolutional layers is de-
signed to extract multi-resolution features of the pose guid-
ance images, and the extracted features are added to the
latent features of the same resolution in the downsample

3.3. Training Strategy
The training of our method has two stages. In the first stage,
the motion generation module is trained on listening head
datasets while the rendering module is pretrained on lis-
tening head dataset together with a large scale talking head
dataset VFHQ [40]. For the rendering module pretrain, we
do not incorporate the speaker information, and the ground
truth head poses and facial expression coefficients are used
as guidance signals. In the second stage, we train the im-
plicit motion refinement part in the rendering backbone. In
this stage, we incorporate the explicit motion generated by
the motion generation module as guidance.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Setup
Dataset. We conduct experiments on the widely used ViCo
dataset [46] and RealTalk dataset [9]. Both ViCo and Re-
alTalk consist of videos of face-to-face daily conversations
between two individuals of diverse ages, genders, and eth-
nicity. We follow the training and testing split provided
in their original papers. ViCo contains 398 clips of total
1.3 hours for training, 43 clips for testing, and 42 clips for
out-of-distribution (OOD) testing. RealTalk contains 1489
clips of total 6.4 hours for training and 407 clips for testing.
Additionally, VFHQ dataset [40], a large-scale high-quality
talking head video dataset containing over 16k clips, is used
to pretrain the rendering module.
Implementation Details. For both datasets, we crop the
original videos into 512 × 512 face videos for training and
testing, and use MediaPipe [23] to extract 51-dim facial ex-
pression coefficients and 6-dim head pose. For the motion
generation module, we train it with the input sequence of
50 frames and a batch size of 64. The diffusion step is set
to 1000 for training and inference. For the high-fidelity ren-
dering, we set the input to 16 frames with a batch size of 8.
The diffusion step is 1000 for training and 25 for inference.
Both modules are trained with AdamW [22] optimizer.

Evaluation Metrics.
We compare both visual and mo-
tion quality for evaluation. The visual quality is evaluated
by Fr´echet Inception Distance (FID) [12], Peak Signal-to-
Noise Ratio (PSNR), and Structural Similarity (SSIM) [38].
For the motion quality evaluation, we adopt 1) Feature Dis-
tance (FD), the L1 distance between the generated motion
and the ground truth motion, 2) FID∆fm [43], the FID score
of motion difference between consecutive frames, and 3)
Sequence Naturalness Distance (SND) [43], the summa-
rization of FID∆fm and FID score of motion. For FID∆fm
and SND, we concatenate the angle and translation to com-
pute the overall pose metrics. To compare with existing
works, we extract the 3DMM coefficients by [8], to com-
pute the motion quality metrics.

the OOD set demonstrates our better generalization ability.
These results verify that our method can largely promote
the visual and motion quality, enabling realistic and vivid
listening head generation.

tion 3.2, we propose to use separate controls of head pose
and facial expression according to the intrinsics of these two
types of motion signals. To validate the effectiveness of the
proposed control strategies, we remove the pose images in
the rendering module, and concatenate the head pose coeffi-
cients with the facial expression coefficients. The concate-
nated motion signal is injected into the rendering module
through the expression cross-attention layer. The results in
Table 4 show that using concatenated motion as the con-
trol signal leads to a degeneration in both motion accuracy
and naturalness, especially on expression metrics. We also
present some visualizations in Fig. 4. From the visualiza-
tion, there exists inaccurate pose control and visual arti-
facts when using concatenated motion signals (the left ex-
ample), and poor expression controllability (the right exam-
ple). These results verify the effectiveness of the proposed
pose and expression controls.
Implicit Motion Refinement.
Furthermore, we validate
the effectiveness of the proposed implicit motion refinement
method. As shown in Table 3, without the refinement mod-
ule, the motion quality and visual quality of the generated
video both degrade. As shown in Fig. 5, we observe mainly
two effects of the implicit motion refinement. The first is
that the refinement fixes some wrong facial expressions (the
left example), and the second is that it tends to add more fa-
cial details to the generated videos, such as more realistic
wrinkles (the right example). As a result, we can observe
more stable and detailed facial expressions after refinement.
This implies that the implicit motion refinement helps the
rendering module learn to revise the details of the gener-
ated videos given additional conditions, further improving
the visual qualities.
Discussions. Our proposed framework can generate high
fidelity listening head videos with accurate motion, vivid
facial details and expressive motions. Nevertheless, there
are still some limitations. 1) The condition used to generate
listening heads is restricted within the speaker’s audio and
facial expressions. But more flexible conditions such as text
description, motion styles etc. are always appreciated. The
proposed hybrid motion generation has the potential of inte-
grating diverse control signals, we will study this in the fu-
ture. 2) Time complexities. The motion generation module
in our framework is lightweight and can generate motions
at 15 fps, the computational bottleneck lies in the rendering
module. This is a common limitation of the LDM-based
methods and can be alleviated by model quantization or dis-
tillation, and we will explore more in the future.
Supplementary Materials. We provide user study, details
of network, training objectives, experimental setups, ethics,
and more visual results in supplementary materials.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a diffusion-based listening head
generation framework that generates photorealistic listen-
ing head videos up to 512 × 512 resolution, with highly
expressive motions and fine facial details. A hybrid motion
modeling method that consists of an explicit motion gener-
ation module, a specially designed motion control strategy
and an implicit motion refining process eases the training
of the LDM-based framework, making the learning on a
small scale dataset feasible. Comprehensive experiments on
Realtalk and ViCo dataset demonstrate that our framework
achieves superior results in terms of visual quality and mo-
tion expressiveness compared with several state-of-the-art
methods. And a thorough ablation study proves the effec-
tiveness and necessity of the proposed hybrid motion mod-
eling method. In the future, we will study further to improve
the controllability and the generating speed of our model.
